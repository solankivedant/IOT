Why this is a bigger and easier market than it looks

The loss is enormous and precisely measured. Post-harvest losses run 6.02–15.05% for fruits and 4.87–11.61% for vegetables, per the NABCONS study commissioned by the Ministry of Food Processing. The aggregate figure: roughly ₹92,000 crore in annual post-harvest losses, against a cold chain market growing 16–25% CAGR. And the exposure isn't distributed evenly across the chain — most harvested produce in India still travels in open trucks or gunny sacks with no cooling at all, and less than 10% of horticultural production ever sees cold storage. 
Ground Report + 2

The failure mode you're solving is invisible, not just costly. The clearest documented case: a reefer's compressor develops a slow refrigerant leak the driver never notices; four hours later the cargo has climbed from 4°C to 14°C and is unsellable, and the failure went undetected because nobody was watching the temperature in real time — a fleet manager tracking only GPS coordinates has no way of knowing the cargo is slowly warming inside a truck that looks perfectly normal from outside. That's not a hypothetical — it's the exact scenario your Layer 3 rate-of-change detection is built to catch before the load is lost.

The subsidy makes your price effectively invisible to the buyer. PMKSY grants 35–50% of eligible project cost, up to a maximum subsidy of ₹10 crore, and refrigerated trucks and insulated vans are explicitly named as an eligible cold-chain component. Eligible applicants include companies, cooperatives, FPOs, SHGs, and NGOs. If your monitoring system is bundled into the reefer vehicle's project cost when a cold-chain operator applies, MoFPI effectively pays for a third to half of your product.

Why hash-chaining, not just logging

Every competitor already logs temperature — the search results above are full of them (Fleetx, Navionyx, Splakdhn, Motive, Tracker Systems). What none of them lead with is tamper-evidence as the sales pitch rather than a technical footnote. Navionyx already markets its digital log chain as "tamper-proof and cloud-stored," which tells you buyers are asking for exactly this — but the implementations are mostly "we don't let you edit it in our UI," not a real cryptographic chain that would hold up under a genuine dispute or audit. 
Navionyx

The reason this matters commercially, not just technically: a temperature log that could have been edited is worth nothing in a dispute. A fleet operator facing a rejected-load claim from a hospital pharmacy needs a record neither party can plausibly claim was altered. Hash-chaining each reading to the previous one — the same primitive as a blockchain, without needing an actual blockchain — is cheap to implement and is the single feature that turns your product from "another GPS tracker with a thermometer" into evidence a court or FSSAI inspector will actually credit.

Revenue model — three tiers, same product

Tier 1: Fleet operator subscription. The dashboard and alerting is the retention hook — this is what keeps the fleet manager logged in daily. Priced per vehicle per month.

Tier 2: Buyer-facing certificate (the actual differentiator). Fleetx already positions temperature reports as "documented proof of service quality that builds trust and justifies premium pricing" for operators serving pharma and hospital clients. Charge the fleet operator extra for QR-verifiable certificates they can hand to buyers — this is what lets a mid-tier transporter compete for pharma and export contracts against bigger players with in-house compliance teams.

Tier 3: PMKSY-linked deployment. Partner with cold-chain operators before they file their PMKSY application, so your system is written into the project cost from day one rather than retrofitted. This is a slower sales cycle but a much bigger one — you're riding a government-subsidized capex cycle instead of selling against price resistance.

Team split for four people

Person 1 — Hardware and firmware. Logger PCB, sensor integration, power management (the backup battery failover logic is the trickiest firmware piece — it has to detect power loss and log it before the battery runs out), tamper-evident enclosure.

Person 2 — Backend and the hash chain. This is the person who owns the actual IP. Implements the signing scheme, the chain verification pipeline, gap detection, and the certificate generation service. Gets this wrong and the whole "tamper-evident" pitch is just marketing copy.

Person 3 — Anomaly detection and fleet dashboard. Builds the excursion detection (threshold + rate-of-change), the live map, and the alerting pipeline. This person should spend real time on the rate-of-change model specifically — a naive threshold-only system misses the exact failure mode (slow compressor leak) that does the most damage.

Person 4 — Fleet operator and compliance relations. Talks to transporters, cold storage operators, and pharma distributors. Learns the PMKSY application process well enough to help a customer's application succeed — that's a service few competitors offer, and it's what gets you into the deal before a competitor's sensor does.

First deployment roadmap

Month 1–2: Build and bench-test the logger on 2 vehicles. Validate against a manual data logger (LogTag or similar) running in parallel — you need to prove your ±0.3°C accuracy claim before anyone trusts your certificate.

Month 3: Deploy on a small transporter's fleet (5–10 vehicles) carrying dairy or produce for a local distributor. Gujarat has strong cold-chain transporter density around Ahmedabad, Anand, and Mehsana — dairy cooperative territory is a natural first customer base given the existing cold-chain discipline in that sector.

Month 4–5: Add the certificate layer and pitch it to one pharma distributor or hospital supply chain — this is where the willingness to pay is highest and the rejected-load pain is most acute.

Month 6: Use the season's data — excursions caught before spoilage, disputes resolved by the log — to approach a MoFPI-registered Program Management Agency (PMA) or Project Management Consultant (PMC) about being named as the recommended monitoring vendor for new PMKSY cold-chain applications in the state.

The single most persuasive number you can generate in your first six months: how many loads were saved because an alert let a driver reroute to the nearest working cold store before the temperature crossed the threshold. That's the number that sells to both fleet operators and to MoFPI.
