Wired temperature probes
2–4 NTC thermistor probes per vehicle — front, rear, and per compartment in multi-zone reefers. Wired, not Bluetooth: zero battery risk on the sensor itself, since a dead sensor battery mid-transit is worse than no sensor at all. ±0.3°C accuracy, rated -40°C to +85°C. ₹400–800 each
Door open/close sensor
Magnetic reed switch logs every open/close event with exact timestamp and GPS coordinate. Core evidence layer — distinguishes a legitimate delivery stop from an unauthorized cargo tamper or a dilution/swap stop. Every event enters the immutable log.
Reefer unit fuel sensor
Independent diesel-powered reefer units (Thermo King, Carrier) run off a separate tank from the truck engine. A capacitive fuel-level sensor flags rapid drops that indicate siphoning or theft — the single most common way operators fake a "compliant" trip while running the reefer half-off to save diesel.
GPS + accelerometer
Standard GNSS module for location and route. Accelerometer flags harsh braking (spoilage risk from impact) and detects when the vehicle is stationary vs moving — used to auto-adjust sample rate (denser logging while moving).

Logger hardware
ESP32 or STM32 base with SIM7600 4G module (GPS + cellular in one chip). Runs on vehicle 12V power via buck converter. ₹2,500–3,500 for the compute + radio module alone, tamper-evident sealed enclosure.
Cryptographic signing at the edge
Each reading is hashed (SHA-256) and chained to the previous reading's hash before leaving the device — the same structure as a blockchain, minus the network overhead. This is the actual product: it means nobody, not the driver, not the fleet operator, not even you, can quietly edit a reading after the fact without breaking the chain. That tamper-evidence is what converts a temperature log into evidence a court or an FSSAI auditor will accept.
Local storage buffer
MicroSD stores 30 days of full-resolution data. Rural stretches lose 4G coverage regularly — the chain must never have gaps. On reconnect, backlogged readings upload with original timestamps preserved, hash-chain intact.
Backup battery
Small LiPo cell keeps the logger alive for 4+ hours if the vehicle battery is disconnected — the exact moment someone might try to defeat the system. If main power cuts, the unit logs a POWER_LOSS event before going dark, so a gap in the log is itself evidence, not a blind spot.
4G MQTT uplink, retries on reconnect

Hash chain verification
On ingestion, the backend re-verifies each reading's hash against the previous one. A broken chain, an out-of-sequence timestamp, or a gap longer than the expected sample interval is flagged and cannot be silently deleted from the record — it becomes a permanent annotation on that trip's report.
Temperature excursion detection
Two triggers: threshold breach — reading outside the consignment's set range (e.g. 2–8°C for vaccines) — and rate-of-change — a temperature climbing 3°C faster than a working compressor should allow, which catches a failing unit before it fully fails. This is the exact failure mode that destroyed an ₹8 lakh pharma consignment in one documented case: a slow refrigerant leak nobody was watching in real time.
Live alerting
Breach triggers simultaneous alert to driver, fleet manager, and operations head via SMS + WhatsApp + in-app push. The driver alert matters most — they're the only one who can act in the next 10 minutes, before the load is already lost.
Fleet dashboard
Live map with temperature overlay per vehicle, historical trip logs, door-event timeline, and fuel-drop flags. Built for the fleet operator's ops team — this is the retention product even before the certificate becomes the sales product.

The cold chain certificate
A one-page PDF, QR-linked to the full verifiable hash chain, handed to the receiving buyer (hospital pharmacy, retail chain, exporter) at delivery: full temperature range for the trip, door-event count, any excursions with duration. This is the product, not the sensor. The hardware is a commodity; a certificate the receiving party trusts enough to skip their own inspection is what they'll actually pay a subscription for.
PMKSY-ready compliance export
MoFPI's Integrated Cold Chain scheme funds refrigerated transport as an explicit eligible component, at 35% (general) to 50% (NE/hill states) of project cost. Your monitoring hardware and subscription can be written into the project cost claimed under this grant — meaning the operator's effective price for your system is roughly half of list, and you export the exact audit trail MoFPI inspection visits ask for.
Rejected-load dispute resolution
When a buyer rejects a load claiming spoilage, the hash-chained log is the tiebreaker: either it shows a genuine excursion (buyer is right, claim proceeds against the carrier) or it shows the load stayed in-range the whole trip (carrier is protected from a false claim). Fleet operators buy this feature to protect revenue, not just to comply.
